## Your Willow Farm records, made answerable — a guide

**For Frances · As of [YYMMDD].**

You already know what's in these folders — you built them. What's changed is that the archive now
**answers questions for you** instead of making you dig. Every system folder opens with a short,
plain-English summary of how that system works, who services it, and what's coming due — distilled
from your own MASTER notes plus the receipts and records. This guide explains what was added and
how to get the most from it.

**Nothing was thrown away.** Your original files are all still there, untouched. Everything new
sits *on top* of them as a navigation layer.

### 1. How the folders are organized now

The contents are the same; a few overlapping piles were tidied so related things live together:
the two old "Heat AC" folders are merged into **HVAC**, the pool and spa papers sit together, and
the build receipts live in **Capital Improvements**. A naming habit was kept consistent: dates
lead (like `20240611_...`), so files line up in order. Your hand-written **MASTER notes remain
the heart of each system.**

One housekeeping folder holds things set aside for you: **`XX_DELETE-MANUALLY`** — exact
duplicates and empty folders, staged for *you* to delete once you've glanced at them. Nothing was
permanently deleted; that is always left to you.

### 2. What's new — the value-added features

**Folder summaries (`_Synthesis.md`).** Almost every folder now contains one. Open it first: at
the top is an "operating quick-reference" — vendor and phone number, how the system works, what's
due next, the known gotchas — followed by the fuller story. Each one reads **every document on
that topic** and compiles them into one picture, so the whole tells you more than any single file
could. A real example: the HVAC summary spotted that **the geothermal loop cylinder must be topped
up every May** — low water took a zone offline in 2024 *and* 2026, a pattern no single service
invoice stated.

**The Operations Hub** (at the top of the folder). One page pulling the cross-cutting essentials
together: a **what's-coming-due calendar** (the single most useful page), a **systems → vendor
directory**, and a **sensitive-information map** (where each code and password lives — never the
values themselves).

**Your MASTER notes are preserved, untouched, and kept current.** The summaries are *derived from*
your notes, so the easy habit is to change them through your AI assistant, which updates note and
summary in one motion — and if you edit a note directly, just mention it afterward so the summary
catches up. Your notes remain the source of truth; nothing was added to or changed in them.

**An inbox for anything new.** Drop new papers, emails, or photos into **`XX_ASSETS-TO-FILE`** and
tell your assistant — it files each item into the right folder and folds the new knowledge into
that folder's summary. Saved email threads are especially valuable: formal documents say *what*
was decided; your emails capture *how and why*.

### 3. Using it through the file system (no software needed)

Open the Operations Hub for "what's due / who do I call," or any folder's `_Synthesis.md` for that
system's story; check the **As of** date at the top to see how current it is. These files are
Markdown (`.md`) — plain text that any editor opens, but far nicer in a Markdown reader (on a Mac,
Marked; on Windows, Typora). The summaries help most exactly when you're in a hurry — a vendor on
the phone, something leaking.

### 4. Using it through your AI assistant (the powerful way)

Just ask, in plain language — look-ups (*"When is the generator due? Who services the well?"*),
compilations (*"List everything due in the next six months"*), updates (*"ValleyAir came and
replaced the loop pump — update the HVAC summary"*), and new documents (*"Draft a one-page summary
of the insurance coverage for our accountant"*). **The one habit worth forming: when something
changes, mention it — a sentence is enough.** That's what keeps the archive self-maintaining.

### 5. Worth your eye

A short list of things that surfaced while reading everything — none emergencies, some
time-sensitive. For example: *the newest insurance declarations on file are from 2023 — current
ones should be added; the [251103] boiler service has no completion record — verify before
heating season.*

### In short

The folders now do three things they didn't before: **summarize themselves**, **tell you what's
coming due**, and **answer questions** — through the files directly, or by just asking. When
something changes, mention it to your assistant — that one habit keeps everything else up to date.

*Questions, or want a summary expanded? Just ask.*
